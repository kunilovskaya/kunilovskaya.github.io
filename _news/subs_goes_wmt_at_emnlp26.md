This is a somewhat delayed announcement that my paper exploring the relationship between translation task difficulty and translationese will be presented at **WMT**, co-located with **EMNLP 2026 in Budapest**.

This paper is my most significant contribution to the B7 project *Translation as Rational Communication*, part of SFB 1102.

I am happy that I managed to push this work through and obtain convincing results by combining information-theoretic measures of cognitive load with more traditional indicators of source-text and cross-lingual transfer difficulty. The key question was:

> **To what extent does translation task difficulty explain the amount of translationese in professional written and spoken translation?**

I investigate this question using parliamentary data for English ↔ German.

The answer? At best, **R² = 21%**. So a lot of variance remains unexplained. But I have seen papers on similar topics where a model explaining 5% of the variance was described as a strong model, so 21% is certainly not negligible.

More interestingly, digging into the individual difficulty indicators revealed a sharp divide between **translation and interpreting** in how incoming difficulty is handled.

Written translation tends to **reproduce difficulty**: more demanding source texts result in more translationese, broadly in line with what we would expect.

In interpreting, however, structural complexity seems to be **dispensed with altogether**, producing a paradoxical inverse relationship: the more structurally demanding the source text, the *less* translationese we observe in the interpreted output.

This contrast, I think, is one of the most interesting findings of the paper.
